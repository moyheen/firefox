Researchers at a Vancouver lab announced this morning that they have developed a new method for recycling lithium-ion batteries that recovers more than ninety-five percent of the cobalt, nickel, and lithium in spent cells. The process uses a water-based chemistry instead of the high-temperature smelting common in industrial recycling, and the team says it could be deployed at small regional facilities rather than at a single centralized plant. A pilot operation is scheduled to begin in the spring of next year. Industry analysts said the approach, if it scales, could meaningfully reduce the environmental cost of battery production.
