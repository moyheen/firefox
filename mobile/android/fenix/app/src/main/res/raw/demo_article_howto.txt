How to brew a better cup of pour-over coffee at home, in five steps.

First, weigh your beans. Most pour-over recipes call for sixty grams of coffee per liter of water, which works out to about fifteen grams for a single cup. A kitchen scale is more reliable than a scoop.

Second, grind just before brewing. Pre-ground coffee loses its character within minutes. For pour-over, aim for a medium-coarse grind, roughly the texture of sea salt.

Third, heat your water to between ninety and ninety-six degrees Celsius. If your kettle does not display a temperature, bring the water to a boil and then let it rest for thirty seconds before pouring.

Fourth, bloom the grounds. Pour just enough water to wet all the coffee, then wait thirty to forty-five seconds. This releases trapped carbon dioxide and produces a more even extraction.

Fifth, pour in slow, steady circles. Start at the center, spiral outward, and avoid pouring directly against the filter. The whole brew should take between three and four minutes.

That is the entire technique. The rest is practice.
